# Phrases to remove

## Throat-clearing openers

Remove these announcement phrases. State the content directly.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what [X]"
- "Here's this [X]"
- "Here's that [X]"
- "Here's why [X]"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let me be clear"
- "The truth is,"
- "I'll say it again:"
- "I'm going to be honest"
- "Can we talk about"
- "Here's what I find interesting"
- "Here's the problem though"

Any "here's what/this/that" construction is throat-clearing before the point. Cut it and state
the point.

## Emphasis crutches

These add no meaning. Delete them.

- "Full stop." / "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "This matters because"
- "Make no mistake"
- "Here's why that matters"

## Business jargon

Replace with plain language.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| Navigate (challenges) | Handle, address |
| Unpack (analysis) | Explain, examine |
| Lean into | Accept, commit to |
| Landscape (context) | Situation, field |
| Game-changer | Name the change |
| Double down | Commit, increase |
| Deep dive | Analysis, examination |
| Take a step back | Reconsider |
| Moving forward | Next, from now |
| Circle back | Return to, revisit |
| On the same page | Aligned, agreed |

## Buzzwords

Each one claims an outcome without naming it. Replace it with the outcome: "Cut weekly
reporting from four hours to fifteen minutes", never "streamline your reporting".

- streamline
- innovative
- seamless
- robust
- leverage
- empower
- unlock
- elevate
- revolutionize
- cutting-edge, game-changing, best-in-class, world-class
- synergy

Two more are often technical and fine there: "optimize images", "transform the data". As
marketing claims ("optimize your workflow", "transform your business") they are buzzwords.

## Plain words

Write for a reader whose first language is not English. A simple word beats a stiff synonym.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help |
| commence | start |
| prior to | before |
| in order to | to |
| cited for | named on, mentioned in |

## Adverbs

Cut -ly adverbs, intensifiers, softeners and hedges. They add emphasis without adding a fact.

Specific offenders:

- "really"
- "very"
- "just"
- "quite"
- "literally"
- "genuinely"
- "honestly"
- "simply"
- "actually"
- "deeply"
- "truly"
- "fundamentally"
- "inherently"
- "inevitably"
- "interestingly"
- "importantly"
- "crucially"

**Keep an adverb that carries a fact.** Deleting it changes what the sentence claims:

- "measured daily" (a cadence)
- "only on the paid plan" (a scope)
- "twice a month" (a count)
- "cancel anytime" (a permission)

The test: delete the adverb and reread. If the sentence now says something different, keep it.
If it says the same thing with less noise, cut it.

Also cut these filler phrases:

- "At its core"
- "In today's [X]"
- "It's worth noting"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"

## Trailing qualifiers

A clause tacked onto the end that adds no fact.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "Cancel any time before it does" | "Cancel anytime" |
| "…if that makes sense" | Delete |
| "…and so on" | Name the rest or stop |

## Hedge connectives

A connective that frames the sentence instead of stating it.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "That is a finding rather than an error" | State what is true: "The import skipped 12 rows with no email." |
| "It is worth mentioning that X" | "X" |
| "It should be noted that X" | "X" |

## A whole phrase standing in for one word

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "What's left this period" | "Usage" |
| "The place where you manage your team" | "Team" |
| "Things you can change about your account" | "Settings" |

## House-tic words

A word used everywhere stops meaning anything. One product put "evidence" in six marketing
strings, two headings and a pane label, and the word became a tic. Each use became the specific
thing it pointed at: the stored answer, the sources, the question behind a page.

Count a recurring word across the whole surface. When one word carries that much traffic,
replace each use with the thing it names.

## Meta-commentary

Remove self-referential asides. The text should move, not announce its own structure.

- "Hint:"
- "Plot twist:" / "Spoiler:"
- "You already know this, but"
- "But that's another post"
- "X is a feature, not a bug"
- "Dressed up as"
- "The rest of this essay explains..."
- "Let me walk you through..."
- "In this section, we'll..."
- "As we'll see..."
- "I want to explore..."

## Performative emphasis

False intimacy or manufactured sincerity:

- "creeps in"
- "I promise"
- "They exist, I promise"

## Telling instead of showing

Announcing difficulty or significance instead of demonstrating it:

- "This is genuinely hard"
- "This is what leadership actually looks like"
- "This is what X actually looks like"
- "actually matters"

## Vague declaratives

Sentences that announce importance without naming the specific thing. Kill these.

- "The reasons are structural"
- "The implications are significant"
- "This is the deepest problem"
- "The stakes are high"
- "The consequences are real"

If a sentence says something is important, deep or structural without showing the specific
thing, cut it or replace it with the specific thing.
