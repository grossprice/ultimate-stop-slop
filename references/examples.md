# Before/after examples

## Example 1: Throat-clearing + binary contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard. Technology is manageable. People aren't."

**Changes:** Removed opener, binary contrast structure, and emphasis crutch. Direct statements.

---

## Example 2: Filler + unnecessary reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

**Changes:** Cut hedging ("most"), removed throat-clearing phrases, deleted permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: Business jargon stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

**Changes:** Eliminated jargon entirely. Core message in five words.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "Speed, quality, cost: pick two."

**Changes:** Single sentence. No performative emphasis. A colon does the job an em dash did in
earlier versions of this example. The three items stay because they are the content.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No rhetorical scaffolding and no "X, not Y" tail.

---

## Example 6: Reader homework

**Before:**
> "You need to review each report before you share it."

**After:**
> "Each report arrives with the sources behind it."

**Changes:** The reader has no task. The sentence says what the product does.

---

## Example 7: Selling by refusal

**Before:**
> "What we will not do. We will not sell your data. We will not spam you. No promise about rankings."

**After:**
> "We store your data in the EU and delete it 30 days after you cancel."

**Changes:** One checkable fact replaces a list of refusals that raised doubts.

---

## Example 8: Servant verb

**Before:**
> "Set your tone once, and every draft obeys it. Edit a link and the list follows."

**After:**
> "We write each draft in the tone you set. We read the links from the draft on every save, so a link you edit shows up here."

**Changes:** The product is the actor. Nothing obeys or follows the user.

---

## Example 9: A label written as a sentence

**Before:**
> Toggle: "Read it as a table" / "Read it as a chart"
> Metric label: "How often customers opened your emails."

**After:**
> Toggle: "Table view" / "Chart view"
> Metric label: "Open rate"

**Changes:** Labels name the thing. The explanation, if needed, goes behind an info hint.

---

## Example 10: A standalone fact as a heading

**Before:**
> "Most buyers compare three vendors."

**After:**
> "Most buyers compare three vendors. Are you on the list?"

**Changes:** The heading carries the fact and the question it creates.

---

## Example 11: Trailing qualifier and reassurance tagline

**Before:**
> Button: "Start trial"
> Under it: "No credit card needed. Cancel any time before it renews. Setup takes minutes."

**After:**
> Button: "Start trial"
> Under it: "Cancel anytime."

**Changes:** One fact survives. The rest was decoration.

---

## Example 12: A plan with an open question

**Before:**
> "## Open questions
> - Should the repo be public or private? TBD.
> - Not sure which name to use; we can decide later."

**After:** Nothing in the plan. Before writing it, ask:
> "Should the new repo be public or private?"
> - Public (Recommended): anyone can install it with one command. Anyone can also read it.
> - Private: only you can see it. Installing needs your login on each machine.

Then the plan states the answer: "Repo: public."

**Changes:** The gap became a question with a recommendation and honest trade-offs. The plan
ships with no unknowns.
